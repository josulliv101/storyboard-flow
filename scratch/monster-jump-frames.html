Opening and closing are no longer the same arc. Both crouch at 17% and leave the ground at 32% and peak at 58% — the opening one is the closing one's shape, foreshortened, because it ends about 1.45× further from the reader. What differs is the fall. The close carries it on a single stop at 82%; the open samples it at six, because an animation-timing-function applies between every PAIR of keyframes, so a long segment front-loads its own motion and then coasts. Carried by two stops the descent measured 24.7°, 9.3, 4.0, 0.1, then 46.0, 23.1, 3.3 — drop, flatten, drop, flatten, arriving almost horizontal. Sampled, each segment is too short for the easing to shape it and the speed grows all the way into contact.

Same creatures, same clock, cropped to the eye — because after the landing almost nothing else is still moving. The body is held still here so the eye stays in frame; every part inside it runs on its real timing. The pupil is thrown along the jump, flicks home in one ballistic move with no overshoot, and only then starts to shrink.
